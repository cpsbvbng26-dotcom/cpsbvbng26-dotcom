One thing about the mathematics is now settled. The operator behind the three Trinity-Infinity papers, x ← DQx + (I−D)p, was already a known model — the Friedkin–Johnsen model (1990 / 1999) in sociology. The opinion-dynamics literature routinely names DeGroot and Friedkin–Johnsen together as the most influential models in that area; it became a Cambridge monograph in 2011, reached Science in 2016, and is now used in control theory and computer science. Not a footnote — one of the two pillars of the field. And Series I names consensus dynamics in its own first section while citing nothing from that field (ERRATA E8, severity high). The dimensions, stated exactly: this operator is the special case where the influence matrix W is a cyclic permutation — a ring in which each party listens to exactly one neighbour. The general W, who influences whom and by how much, which is what makes Friedkin–Johnsen sociology, is dropped entirely. What was rediscovered is the mathematical frame of a first-rank sociological model, with the sociology taken out, and its load-bearing term.

One thing has been added beyond that. It is a continuation of first-year work (Trinity-Infinity). On 2026-10-04 a note was written showing that the same operator decides the equilibria of a repeated game (trinity-operator, games). The continuation values of a cyclic path of play are the fixed point of this operator. The formula itself is the textbook one. From it, thresholds were proved: a path on which three players take turns defecting is an equilibrium exactly when the discount factor is at least 1/φ. The note also shows that the Series II example (2,4,2) is not sustainable for any discount factor. The propositions have not been checked against the literature. They may be known exercises, and they are not counted as new results until they are checked. The note also states a conjecture: without randomization, the set of sustainable payoffs is full of holes. It is called the golden-window conjecture, the window being the discount factors from 1/φ to 2/3. The name describes the statement and is not a person’s name. The conjecture and what is proved outside the window are set out on a page of their own. There is no proof, and the evidence is numerical only. The known model, the proved propositions and the conjectures are written down separately.

Claim

One claim, chosen because it is the easiest thing here to check. Agreeing or refuting it takes a browser. This claim is about software (errata-check).

A published artifact cannot be revised. Only the errata can. So the errata drifts away from its source over time. A quotation loses a word, occurrences are undercounted, an item declared unresolved is quietly marked resolved. That drift can be caught by machine, without inference.

What the three papers came to
The framework did not survive. What remains is one fact about one operator: for Q an n-cycle and D diagonal, (DQ)ⁿ = (∏ᵢ aᵢ)·I exactly, so convergence is governed by the geometric mean of the coefficients — not by their maximum, and not by the operator norm.The fact is correct and machine-checked. Its level is that of a second- or third-year undergraduate exercise. The error was the one confusion that range puts on its exam — which is exactly why it could be turned into a check that will never let the same confusion through again. Only the tool used to repair the proof belongs to graduate linear systems and matrix analysis: the transient growth of a non-normal matrix (the error swells 11.8× at step 2) and the construction of a contracting norm from a Lyapunov equation. The level of the tool and the level of what it repaired are two different things. The closest department is not numerical analysis but linear systems and control: the object is the asymptotic stability of a discrete-time system. Of the symbol Ⅲ∞, the half with mathematical content (Ⅲ, three) is the half that was proved not to matter. The route is recorded here.
Whose claim this is
The tool was not built first and then pointed at a paper. It came out of auditing my own three papers. The undercount, the characters added to a quotation that are not in the original, and the last-updated stamp that was older than a date inside the document were all found in my own records.
Evidence
I believed the three papers promised a bundled verification script in three places. Recounted, there are six. That was found by machine, not by reading. 109 checks on the errata itself, 82 on the tool. The tool is checked by breaking a passing state in 30 distinct ways and asserting that each break fails exactly the corresponding check.
The outside eye
The frozen papers are run through the Stanford Agentic Reviewer (paperreview.ai, built by Yixing Jiang and Andrew Ng), and every point it raises is checked one by one and recorded. What it got right and what it got wrong are both kept.It is not peer review. It grounds its reviews in arXiv, so its aim is weaker in this field. That caveat is part of what is being measured — it is recorded alongside the reviews (in Japanese). One review independently found E6; another invented a typo that does not exist.
What would refute it
(1) Show one case that passes these checks while the errata does in fact diverge from its source. (2) Or show one check that does not fail when broken as declared. Either one refutes it. The declaration format and the break tests are in errata-check.

The volume of verification says nothing about the value of what is verified. No check in this system can decide that question. It is falsifiable at the level of claims and structurally unfalsifiable at the level of significance. That asymmetry does not go away. Rather than let it look as though it had, it is recorded as unresolved (ST-002).

Site / operator

Sophaxis

An overlay preprint server for philosophy and mathematics. Authors submit work deposited in Zenodo, Figshare, arXiv, SSRN and other repositories by DOI and ORCID iD, and it is listed after moderation. Moderation is not peer review, and quality is not guaranteed. ORCID confirms that the submitter is an author. Work made with generative models is accepted when the use is disclosed. It charges nothing, to authors or to readers.

Open Python / MIT / with a DOI

errata-check

Audits an errata document against a frozen, already-published artifact. Every quotation verbatim, no undercounting, no quiet resolution of an item declared unresolved, and the artifact itself unchanged. Recomputation of p from printed test statistics, the GRIM test, ORCID/ISBN/ISSN check digits and Japanese era years can also be declared. No inference is used in the judgement, so the output does not need to be re-verified by a human.

deposit-check

Checks the DOI metadata of a researcher’s works against their ORCID record. It reports missing abstracts, licences, ORCID iDs and links between versions, and works missing from the ORCID record. Run the operator

A page that iterates the operator x ← DQx + (I−D)p from the three papers above. Type in a matrix and it reports both the spectral radius and the operator norm, judges convergence and monotone decay separately, and draws the fixed point and the error curve. Where the papers' argument breaks, it constructs a distance in which f is a contraction and rebuilds the argument until Banach's theorem applies literally. Eigenvalues, singular values and the Lyapunov equation are all solved inside the page. No outbound requests.
