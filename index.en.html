Claim

One claim, chosen because it is the easiest thing here to check. Agreeing or refuting it takes a browser.

A published artifact cannot be revised. Only the errata can. So the errata drifts away from its source over time. A quotation loses a word, occurrences are undercounted, an item declared unresolvable is quietly marked resolved. That drift can be caught by machine, without inference.

What the three papers came to
The framework did not survive. What remains is one fact about one operator: for Q an n-cycle and D diagonal, (DQ)ⁿ = (∏ᵢ aᵢ)·I exactly, so convergence is governed by the geometric mean of the coefficients — not by their maximum, and not by the operator norm.The fact is correct, machine-checked, and at the level of a second- or third-year undergraduate exercise. The error is the confusion that course sets on its exam. Only the tool used to repair the proof belongs to graduate linear systems and matrix analysis: the transient growth of a non-normal matrix (the error swells 11.8× at step 2) and the construction of a contracting norm from a Lyapunov equation. The level of the tool and the level of what it repaired are two different things. The closest department is not numerical analysis but linear systems and control: the object is the asymptotic stability of a discrete-time system. Of the symbol Ⅲ∞, the half with mathematical content (Ⅲ, three) is the half that was proved not to matter. The route is recorded here.
Whose claim this is
The tool was not built first and then pointed at a paper. It came out of auditing my own three papers. The undercount, the characters added to a quotation that are not in the original, and the last-updated stamp that was older than a date inside the document were all found in my own records.
Evidence
I believed the three papers promised a bundled verification script in three places. Counting the pages, there are six. That was found by machine, not by reading. 83 checks on the errata itself, 74 on the tool. The tool is checked by breaking a passing state in 28 distinct ways and asserting that each break fails exactly the corresponding check.
The outside eye
The frozen papers are run through the Stanford Agentic Reviewer (paperreview.ai, built by Yixing Jiang and Andrew Ng), and every point it raises is checked one by one and recorded. What it got right and what it got wrong are both kept.It is not peer review. It grounds its reviews in arXiv, which puts these papers on the side where its accuracy is lower — that caveat is recorded alongside the reviews (in Japanese). One review independently found E6; another invented a typo that does not exist.
What would refute it
(1) Show one case that passes these checks while the errata does in fact diverge from its source. (2) Or show one check that does not fail when broken as declared. Either one refutes it. The declaration format and the break tests are in errata-check.

The volume of verification says nothing about the value of what is verified. No check in this system can decide that question. However many pass, none of them can ever report that the work is not worth doing. It is falsifiable at the level of claims and structurally unfalsifiable at the level of significance. That is recorded as unresolved (ST-002).

errata-check

Audits an errata document against a frozen, already-published artifact. Every quotation verbatim, no undercounting, no quiet resolution of an item declared unresolvable, and the artifact itself unchanged. Recomputation of p from printed test statistics, the GRIM test, ORCID/ISBN/ISSN check digits and Japanese era years can also be declared. No inference is used in the judgement, so the output does not need to be re-verified by a human.

DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22649899

View on GitHub Record / MIT

self-correction

Every claim I have published that turned out to be wrong, was withdrawn, or cannot be fixed — recorded one by one and never deleted. 78 entries so far. Five statuses only; standing claims must carry a refutation route and unresolvable ones must say why. Identifiers can never be removed: the check walks the git history and fails if any entry that was ever published has since disappeared.

Run the operator

A page that iterates the operator x ← DQx + (I−D)p from the three papers above. Type in a matrix and it reports both the spectral radius and the operator norm, judges convergence and monotone decay separately, and draws the fixed point and the error curve. Where the papers' argument breaks, it constructs a distance in which f is a contraction and rebuilds the argument until Banach's theorem applies literally. Eigenvalues, singular values and the Lyapunov equation are all solved inside the page. No outbound requests.

Open PDF / CC BY 4.0

trinity-infinity

The three papers above and their verification. 15 checks that reimplement the theorems independently and 32 that recompute every number printed on the page. It also records what was established and what was withdrawn.

View on GitHub Python / MIT

trinity-operator

The operator from the three papers above, implemented without restricting Q to a permutation or the coefficients to a uniform scalar. Convergence is governed by the spectral radius; the operator-norm condition the papers impose is stronger than it needs to be. 22 checks, including an example whose error swells to 11.8× before it converges, 51 more on the construction that repairs the broken argument, and 63 on a staged outlook that drops one assumption at a time.
